# Adventure Concepts

Three candidate adventures for TAS Neo. Each is self-contained and can be developed independently. They vary in scale, tone, and mechanical focus so the authoring pipeline gets exercised across different styles.

---

## 1. The Sunken City of Varek

**Scale**: Standard (~300–500 sections)
**Tone**: Claustrophobic, eerie, resource-pressured

### Premise

Three centuries ago the coastal city of Varek vanished beneath the sea in a single night — taken down by a cataclysm that no survivor could explain. The city's ruin is now a legend. You are a salvage diver hired by a merchant consortium to retrieve a stolen relic — the Compass of Varek, an artefact said to navigate currents that flow beneath the known world. The consortium's advance scout descended two weeks ago and never surfaced.

### Core Conflict

Something lives in the ruins. The city did not simply sink — it was pulled down deliberately, and whatever did the pulling has had three centuries to settle in. The longer you stay below, the more the ruins seem aware of your presence.

### Resource Mechanic

Air is the primary resource. The player carries a finite air supply (tracked as a stat) that depletes with every move and drains faster in enclosed spaces. Air caches are hidden in the ruins. Running out is instant death.

### Chapters

| # | Chapter | Setting | Key gate |
|---|---------|---------|----------|
| 1 | The Harbour Ruins | Collapsed docks, flooded warehouses, scavenging | Find a passage into the submerged interior |
| 2 | The Market District | Flooded streets, merchant houses, first signs of life | Obtain a light source that works underwater |
| 3 | The Temple Quarter | Sunken shrine, undead custodians, forbidden archives | Decipher the ritual that sank the city |
| 4 | The Drowned Palace | Royal chambers, the court of the drowned, the betrayer | Retrieve the Compass |
| 5 | The Ascent | Escape routes collapse behind you, the city fights back | Reach the surface alive |

### Key Characters

- **The Advance Scout** — found early, badly injured, can give partial map and warning
- **The Keeper** — a custodian creature that has maintained the ruins for centuries; neutral, can be bargained with or fought
- **The Merchant Contact** — waiting on the surface; motivations are not what they appear

### Key Items

- Air Flask (consumable, restores air supply)
- Waterproof Lantern (required for deep sections)
- Compass of Varek (objective item, powerful navigation artefact)
- The Betrayer's Seal (reveals the true history of the sinking)

## 2. The Iron Road

**Scale**: Short (~100–200 sections)
**Tone**: Fast-paced, politically charged, morally grey

### Premise

You witness the assassination of a royal courier on the Iron Road, the great trade highway connecting three kingdoms. The courier's sealed satchel falls at your feet. The killers — professional, disciplined — turn toward you. You run. The satchel contains a message that someone powerful wants destroyed before it reaches the capital. You do not know what it says. You do not know which side is right.

### Core Conflict

Every faction on the road wants the satchel: the assassins who answer to an unnamed lord, a border spy-ring that intercepted word of the courier's mission, and the royal intelligence service that sent the courier in the first place. Alliances shift. Trusting the wrong person ends the run.

### Mechanical Focus

Choices have political weight. The player accumulates a hidden `suspicion` score that determines which factions are hostile by the time they reach the capital. Bribing guards, travelling under false identity, and using back roads lower suspicion; confrontations and forced crossings raise it.

### Chapters

| # | Chapter | Setting | Key gate |
|---|---------|---------|----------|
| 1 | The Ambush Site | Roadside, forest, first flight | Escape the immediate pursuit |
| 2 | The Border City | Frontier town, inns, corrupt officials, spy contacts | Cross the border into the second kingdom |
| 3 | The Second Kingdom | Open road, noble estates, relay station | Decide who — if anyone — to trust with the satchel |
| 4 | The Capital | City streets, docks, the palace district | Deliver (or destroy, or sell) the message |

### Key Characters

- **The Spymaster** — offers protection at a price; the price grows as the story continues
- **The Border Guard Captain** — corrupt but not cruel; can be bribed or bypassed
- **The Courier's Contact** — the intended recipient; may or may not still be alive
- **The Lord's Agent** — the assassins' handler; appears personally in the final chapter

### Key Items

- The Sealed Satchel (quest item; cannot be opened without consequences)
- Border Pass (forged or legitimate, determines border crossing options)
- Courier's Token (proves legitimacy to royal agents; also marks the bearer as a target)

## 3. The Vault of the Nine

**Scale**: Standard (~300–500 sections)
**Tone**: Classic Fighting Fantasy — puzzle-forward, escalating threat, each floor distinct

### Premise

Four centuries ago nine warlocks — the Nine Wardens — sealed a nameless entity beneath their tower. Each warden took responsibility for one floor of the vault, leaving behind a guardian and a mechanism to maintain the seal. The warlocks are long dead. The seal is cracking. A scholar's dying message reaches you: someone is breaking the seal from inside. You must descend through all nine floors, defeat or appease each guardian, and re-forge the seal before whatever is beneath escapes.

### Core Conflict

The entity below is intelligent and has been manipulating events for decades. One of the Nine's descendants is working willingly to free it, believing the entity's promises of power. The player may not realise this until mid-descent. Each guardian knows a fragment of the truth.

### Floor Design

Each floor has a distinct elemental or conceptual theme, its own mechanics, and a puzzle or combat sequence to claim the floor's seal fragment.

| Floor | Theme | Guardian | Mechanic |
|-------|-------|----------|----------|
| 1 | Stone | The Warden's Stone Golem | Structural — rooms shift, find the key path |
| 2 | Ice | The Frozen Court | Endurance — cold drains STAMINA each section |
| 3 | Illusion | The Mirror Twins | Deduction — one guardian lies, one tells truth |
| 4 | Fire | The Ember Drake | Resource — fire spreads, limited paths |
| 5 | Shadow | The Unbound | Stealth — combat at disadvantage, avoidance rewarded |
| 6 | Water | The Tide Warden | Tide cycle — some sections only passable at ebb |
| 7 | Storm | The Tempest Judge | Trial — answer three questions correctly |
| 8 | Death | The Last Warden | Sacrifice — costs HP to progress; guardians prior floors can help |
| 9 | Void | The Hollow | Final — uses all items, knowledge, and allies gathered |

### Key Characters

- **The Scholar's Ghost** — tutorial and lore device; can be consulted once per floor
- **The Traitor Descendant** — appears on floor 5; can be fought, reasoned with, or recruited
- **The Nine Wardens** (as memory fragments) — each floor reveals one warden's story
- **The Nameless Entity** — communicates through the cracks in the seal; makes offers throughout the descent

### Key Items

- Seal Fragments 1–9 (collected per floor, combined at the bottom)
- Warden's Codex (found on floor 1, fills in lore as floors are completed)
- The Traitor's Key (opens a shortcut or reveals the entity's true weakness)
- Void Glass (floor 9 only — reveals illusions, required for the final confrontation)

## Comparison

| | Sunken City of Varek | The Iron Road | Vault of the Nine |
|-|---------------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Scale | Standard | Short | Standard |
| Primary challenge | Exploration + resource | Political choices | Puzzles + combat |
| Mechanical novelty | Air supply stat | Suspicion score | Per-floor mechanics |
| Replayability | High (multiple routes) | High (faction paths) | Medium (fixed floors) |
| Authoring complexity | High (5 chapters + grids) | Low (4 linear chapters) | Medium (9 floors, modular) |
| Good first adventure? | No — grids add complexity | Yes | Yes |
